Buying guide

Not sure which coffee to choose? Here’s how to read our cards and pick a bag you’ll enjoy.

01

Start with how you brew

Every coffee on our shelf is a light roast, made for pour-over and other filter brewing, where its fruit and florals come through clearly. They also make bright, lively espresso if you like a lighter shot.

02

Choose a process

The process, shown in the corner of every card, shapes the cup as much as the origin. Washed coffees taste clean and bright, with clear acidity. Naturals, dried in the cherry, are sweeter and fruitier, with more body. Anaerobic, dark-room dried and cold-fermented naturals push that further, toward ripe, heady fruit.

03

Read the tasting notes

Tasting notes describe what a coffee reminds us of, such as jasmine, orange or white peach. Nothing is added: the flavours come from the variety, the farm and the processing. Pick the notes that sound most like what you enjoy.

04

Know your Geisha

Geisha is a variety famous for its floral, tea-like delicacy. It grows slowly and yields little, so it costs more and comes in smaller 75g bags. If you’re new to it, a Geisha is a memorable place to start.

05

Buy what you’ll drink

A 100g bag makes about six cups at 16g each, and a 75g bag about four or five. Our coffees taste best 13 to 15 days after the roast date, so buy what you’ll finish in the few weeks after that.

06

Store it well

Keep beans in their bag or an airtight container, somewhere cool and dark, and grind just before brewing. Keep them out of the fridge, where they pick up moisture and smells.

Still not sure?

Ask us at the café, or save a few coffees to your wishlist and compare their cards side by side.
