# Brief: a new version of the torn-paper assets

You're making a different take on the torn-paper artwork for the Lights
Landing Coffee website (this repo: a static site, no build step). The current
version is generated by `tools/torn_paper.py`. Beat it on realism, but keep
every hard requirement below — the site's CSS depends on them.

## What the assets are and how the site uses them

| File (in `uploads/`) | Size | What it is | How the CSS uses it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `torn-band` | 2400×260 | A whole strip of **grey paper**, torn along **both** long edges, in full colour | The site header. Painted as a background stretched to the bar (`100% 100%`), ~1200×130 CSS px on desktop, down to ~390×96 on phones |
| `torn-bottom` | 2400×H | A sheet's bottom edge: paper above, tear along the bottom. **White** paper | Its **alpha** is a CSS `mask-image` that cuts the bottom edge of every page section |
| `torn-bottom-tex` | 2400×H | Greyscale relief for `torn-bottom` (same tear, same alpha) | Drawn over the section with `background-blend-mode: multiply`, so it can only **darken**: white = no change |
| `torn-top`, `torn-top-tex` | 2400×H | The same pair for a top edge (paper below, tear on top) | Not used by the page today, but kept as part of the kit |

`H` is the edge height. It is currently 64px (32 CSS px). You may change
it, but if you do, set `--torn-height` in `assets/styles.css` to `H / 2`px.

Every asset ships **twice**: `.png` (lossless master for design tools) and
`.webp` (what the site loads). Keep the file names exactly.

## The look to aim for

Real paper, torn by hand, photographed under soft light from above.

- **A wide, white, rolled lip** along the tear. Where paper tears, the
  surface layer pulls back and exposes the whiter fibrous core, which rolls
  up slightly. The lip varies in width along the edge (wide in places,
  almost nothing where the tear was clean) and has **soft grey creases**
  inside it that wander and fade. They must not run as parallel ribbons.
- **A fibrous fringe** past the outer edge: a fine fuzz of short, pale,
  semi-transparent fibres, plus the odd longer strand. Not eyelashes.
- **An irregular tear line**: long gentle wander plus sharp small jags,
  never flat or ruled.
- **Paper texture** on the grey strip: fine vertical laid-paper ribbing that
  comes and goes, short fibres, cloudy pulp flocs, gentle mottling, slightly
  uneven lighting. Neutral grey, around RGB 218.
- **Soft shading.** Shadows are paper-grey, never foil-dark or black. A drop
  shadow is added by CSS, so don't bake one in below the paper.

**Visual reference.** The original look came from a stock image that can't
be used commercially. It is in git history and may be looked at but **not
copied, traced or sampled**:

```bash
git show 7ba9627:uploads/torn-band.png > /tmp/ref-band.png        # 720×193
git show 7ba9627:uploads/torn-bottom-tex.png > /tmp/ref-edge.png
```

The current generated version, the one to beat, is what's in `uploads/` now.

## Hard requirements

1. **Original artwork only.** Procedural code, or something you generate
   from scratch. No stock images, no photos you don't own, no tracing of the
   reference. If you use an image-generation model, say so, and note its
   licence terms in the README.
2. **Transparent background, no black anywhere under the alpha.** Every
   pixel, including fully transparent ones and half-transparent fibres, must
   carry paper colour, not black. Black under alpha shows up as a dark
   fringe when the image is scaled or used outside a mask. Use straight
   (un-premultiplied) alpha, and save WebP with `exact=True` (Pillow) or
   `-exact` (cwebp). Otherwise the encoder may blacken "invisible" pixels.
3. **Seamless inner side.** On the edge files, the two rows on the paper's
   inner side (the top rows of `*-bottom*`, the bottom rows of `*-top*`) are
   fully opaque. On the `-tex` files they are pure white (255; WebP within 3),
   so the strip joins the flat sheet with no line.
4. **No tiling.** Each file spans the full 2400px once, with no visible
   repeat.
5. **`torn-top` / `torn-bottom` are white paper** (RGB 255) with the tear in
   the alpha. They work as a mask, an `<img>`, or a background.
6. **Readable header.** Header links are `#5a5249` on the grey strip and
   need at least 4.5:1 contrast: the darkest 1% of the strip's middle half
   must still clear that. Keep the paper light and the texture gentle there.
7. **Weight.** The three files the site loads (`torn-band.webp`,
   `torn-bottom.webp`, `torn-bottom-tex.webp`) total **150 KB or less**.
   About 115 KB today; lossy WebP q≈90 with lossless alpha works well for
   grain.

## How to check your work

```bash
python3 tools/check_torn_paper.py                # every hard requirement
python3 -m http.server 8000                      # then open the pages
```

Look at `index.html` (the header, and the torn edges between sections) at a
1280px-wide window and at 390px (phone), ideally at 2× pixel density.
`product.html` and `cafe.html` show more section edges. Compare against the
reference above and against the current version.

## Scope

- Change only `tools/` (edit `torn_paper.py` or write your own generator,
  but keep it runnable with a documented command), the ten
  `uploads/torn-*` files, `--torn-height` in `assets/styles.css` if the
  edge height changes, and the "Regenerating the torn paper" section of
  `README.md`.
- Don't touch any other page, style or asset.
